13 · Ethical SEO Guidelines

Avoid shortcuts that erode the entire digital asset.

Unethical SEO may look cheap and fast, but it almost always ends in penalties, deindexing, or brand damage that is extremely difficult to recover from.

This is a complete breakdown of SEO practices that must not be done. The explanation is designed to be easy to understand so that website owners realize the major risks lurking behind SEO shortcuts.

01 — Unethical Practices

Any deliberate manipulation intended to deceive search engine algorithms to achieve high rankings instantly, without providing real added value to users.

  • Keyword Stuffing — excessive and unnatural repetition of keywords.
  • Cloaking — different content shown to bots vs. real visitors.
  • Hidden Text / Links — text invisible to humans, readable by bots.
RiskManual penalties up to full deindexing. Brand credibility damaged by poor user experience.

02 — Renting Links

The most common tactic by SEO service providers: fast results while effectively holding the owner hostage to keep paying monthly fees.

  • Paying news sites, blogs, or spam directories to host your link during the rental period.
  • When payment stops, the link is removed immediately.
RiskViolation of Link Spam Policies. Rankings collapse the moment it is detected. Budget wasted on results that do not last.

03 — Black Hat SEO

Techniques that blatantly violate Google Search Essentials, exploiting security loopholes or algorithm weaknesses for instant ranking gains.

  • PBN — a self-made network of sites linking to each other to manufacture fake authority.
  • Scraped Content — copying content from other sites at scale without permission.
  • Automated Content — thousands of bot-written articles without human curation.
RiskThe fastest path to permanent ban. Recovery is slow, painful, and often means rebuilding the site from scratch.
Strategic · Note

Safe SEO always focuses on user experience.

Content relevance and organic authority-building (White Hat SEO) are the only paths that last. Shortcuts may deliver results within weeks — but they almost always end in financial loss and long-term damage to digital assets.
